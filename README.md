# AI Marg: a free AI & ML course site

**AI Marg** takes a complete beginner from their first line of Python to building LLM apps and AI agents, using the best **free** videos on YouTube, NPTEL and other platforms. **Indian teachers come first.** Every module's best pick is from an Indian teacher, in Hindi, Hinglish or Indian English (for example CampusX, Krish Naik, Codebasics and NPTEL/IIT professors); the three languages are treated equally. Global English classics such as StatQuest, 3Blue1Brown and Stanford appear as extra options.

The site is interactive:

- **Curated video lessons** for every module: one "best pick" from an Indian teacher (Hindi, Hinglish and Indian English are treated equally), then other options. Every YouTube video plays inside the site, videos you have really watched (most of each, not skipped through) tick themselves, and playlists remember where you stopped. A playlist shared by several modules is ticked by hand for each module's part, and a course on another site is never ticked by its YouTube intro.
- **27 interactive labs, one for every module.** Drag, click and move sliders to *see* how a first model learns, Python running line by line, Git branches, pandas group-by and SQL joins, gradient descent, overfitting, decision trees, K-Means, neural networks, a PyTorch training loop, convolution filters, embeddings, tokenizers, LLM sampling, RAG search, LoRA, diffusion, AI agents, data drift, Q-learning and statistical significance. Each lab has tasks, and each was checked by a second reviewer who recomputed its numbers.
- **Python in the browser.** Auto-graded coding exercises run on the page using [Pyodide](https://pyodide.org). Nothing to install.
- **Quizzes and level tests.** Each module has a 10-question quiz with explanations, and each level ends with a tougher 20-question test (pass mark 70%).
- **Tough projects.** Each project has a client-style brief, Indian datasets, milestones, a rubric and stretch goals, ending with two grand capstones.
- **374 original diagrams**, at least one on every lesson card (all 189), drawn for this course with Indian examples and checked by a second reviewer who recomputed the numbers. They follow light and dark mode, fill the card's width on phones, and load only when a module is opened.
- **Publish and share projects:** upload a finished project to your own GitHub repository from the project page (with a README written for you), then share it on LinkedIn with a ready-made post.
- **Lesson cards in simple English.** Each module's reading is split into short cards (the big idea, goals, study plan, key terms, an Indian example, common mistakes and self-check tasks), written in short sentences with every technical word explained where it is used, and harder words also given in Hindi, like "average (औसत, ausat)". Every key term has a plain meaning, an everyday comparison and an example (a line of code for coding terms). Every card has at least 5 Indian examples (1,204 in all). Press **Got it** after each card; the ticks count towards the module's progress.
- **Listen (read aloud) everywhere.** A Listen button on every lesson card, page section, key term, glossary term and quiz question, and a speaker button at the top that reads any page from where you are. It uses the voices already on your phone or computer (an Indian English voice when there is one, and a Hindi voice for the Hindi words), so it is free and works offline on most phones. A small player lets you pause, skip, change speed and pick a voice; the paragraph being read is highlighted.
- **A Study room built on research-backed learning methods and Vedic study practices:** Daily review (spaced repetition, Smaraṇa), recall and Why? checks on lesson cards, a Reflect tab per module (Feynman box, Manana questions, Japa rounds, Nididhyāsana), Śravaṇa listening cues and summaries, focus sessions (Dhāraṇā, with an optional Dhyāna breath and a Vairāgya distraction-free mode), a Svādhyāya journal, mixed practice with confidence ratings, a mistakes notebook, a knowledge map with next steps, and a **How to learn** page that explains all 40 methods and how strong the evidence is for each.
- **Play mode: the same course as a game.** A second way to learn the same lessons, as short rounds on a road trip called *The AI Road*, with points, stamps and badges. Open it with the **Play mode** button on Home, the **Play this module** button on any module page, or the **Menu** on phones and tablets. See [Play mode](#play-mode) below.
- **Watch mode: just watch.** A third way: every video the module notes recommend, from Level 1 (beginner) to Level 4 (advanced), one after another in one player. Press play once, and the next video starts by itself. Open it with the **Watch mode** button on Home (**Continue watching** once you have started), the **Watch this module** button on any module page, or the **Menu** on phones and tablets. See [Watch mode](#watch-mode) below.
- **Reminders:** a gentle study reminder at a time you choose, as a notification on your phone (Android, or iPhone and iPad from the Home Screen) or in your calendar. See [Reminders](#reminders) below.
- **Progress tracking, notes, a study planner and a jargon glossary** in plain English. Everything is saved in your own browser, with optional Google sign-in to back up and sync progress across devices: press **Sign in** at the top right (on phones also in the **Menu**). The first Google sign-in creates your free account, so there is no separate sign-up form or password. To sign out, tap the round letter at the top right and press **Sign out**. No ads and no tracking. If you turn on phone reminders, our reminder service keeps your device's notification address, your reminder settings (time, days, time zone, which page to open, whether to skip days you studied, and the last day you studied, as that browser knows it), when it last tried to send you a reminder or skipped one because you had studied, when the next one is due, when you turned reminders on and last saved them, and how many sends failed and tests you sent; **Turn off** deletes them. To stop misuse it also counts requests by a code made from your internet address, deleted within a few hours.

**What's inside:** 4 levels, 27 modules, 428 free resources (409 with verified links), 350 quiz questions, 81 auto-graded coding exercises, 27 labs, 18 projects and 180 glossary terms. The lesson cards are in simple English, with Hindi words in brackets for harder terms, a diagram and at least 5 examples on every card; the videos are in Hindi, Hinglish and Indian English.

## Play mode

Play mode shows the same course as a road trip called **The AI Road**. The lessons, quizzes and saved progress are exactly the same as in Learn; only the way they are shown changes.

- **Each level is a road, and each module is a Stop.** A Stop has a stamp for each part of the module: Read, Watch, Lab, Code and Quiz.
- **Short rounds:**
  - a *card round* shows one lesson card, then a *quick check* asks its question, answered from memory;
  - a *term match* pairs the module's key terms with their meanings;
  - the module quiz is a *toll gate*, whose barrier lifts at 70%;
  - each level test is a *checkpoint*, and passing it stamps a **Road permit**;
  - a *warm-up* reviews the cards that are due (the same cards as Daily review);
  - *quick questions* bring back one past mistake and a couple of questions from earlier stops.
- **XP (experience points)** come from real learning: answering from memory, passing quizzes, and finishing labs, exercises, videos and projects. Pressing **Got it** on its own earns nothing, and doing something again earns nothing new.
- **Goals, ranks and badges:**
  - a small daily goal (Light 20, Steady 30 or Strong 50 XP);
  - a week line, where 3 learning days make a steady week;
  - 10 ranks shown as vehicles, from Starter to Guide;
  - 26 badges.
  Ranks and badges never go down.
- **Kind by design.** There are no lives, no leaderboards, no timers unless you switch one on, and no messages about losing anything. Missing a day costs nothing. **Calm mode** turns off all motion, and it is on by itself when your phone or computer is set to reduce motion.
- **One progress.** Anything you do in Play counts in Learn, and the other way round. When you sign in, it syncs between devices like the rest of your progress. Learn pages opened from Play (a lab, a video, a project) show a **Back to Play** bar.

## Watch mode

Watch mode is for learners who only want to watch. It plays every video that the module notes recommend, from Level 1 (beginner) to Level 4 (advanced), one after another: press play once, and when a video ends the next one starts by itself. There is nothing else to do: no reading, quizzes or labs.

- **The page.** How many videos you have watched and about how many hours are left; the player, with **Previous**, **Next**, **Mark as watched**, **Open on YouTube** and an **Autoplay** switch; and every video of the course, level by level. Open a module to see its videos and play any of them.
- **How long it is.** 1,345 videos, about 738 hours, because it plays every course a module recommends, not only its main pick (the Python module alone has four full Python courses). Press **Next** to skip a video you do not need: it stays unwatched in the list, and you can come back to it.
- **The end of a video.** With Autoplay on, the next video starts after 5 seconds (**Cancel** stops it). With Autoplay off, press **Play next**.
- **Courses on other sites** (Coursera, NPTEL, Kaggle and others, and two whole YouTube channels) cannot play here. When the path reaches one, the player shows a card with what the course costs (for Coursera, how to join for free): open the course in a new tab, and tick **I finished this** when you are done, or **Skip for now**. Autoplay stops there.
- **A video that cannot play here** (its owner allows YouTube only, or it was removed): the player says so, with **Open on YouTube** and **Skip to next**. With Autoplay on, once you are watching, it skips after 8 seconds (**Cancel** stops it, and so does **Open on YouTube**: watch it there, then press **Mark as watched**). If its owner allows YouTube only, the list then marks it "plays on YouTube only" on this device.
- **How it counts.** A video counts as watched when most of it (80%) was really played here (skipping ahead does not count, the same rule as Learn's player), or when you press **Mark as watched** (press it again to undo). It also counts when the video's course is ticked in Learn for that module. When every video of a course in a module is watched, Watch mode ticks that course in Learn for you, the same tick as **I finished this** on the module page. A course on its own site that plays here only through one YouTube video, such as its set-up video, is never ticked for you: tick it yourself when you finish the course, as in Learn. Watch mode never un-ticks Learn by itself.
- **Your place.** Watch mode remembers the video you were on and the second you stopped at, so it carries on from there. It also remembers where you stopped in the last 200 videos you played: go back to one (**Previous**, or **Play** in the list) and it starts there, and what you watched of it before still counts. If you watch the same video on two devices before they sync, Watch mode keeps the place from the device where you watched it last, and only the parts watched there count: if you did finish the video, press **Mark as watched**. Just opening a module's videos (**Watch this module**) does not move your place until you play something there. A few minutes of watching count as a study day, like Learn's player. Missing a day costs nothing.
- **One progress.** What you watch is saved with the rest of your progress, and syncs between devices when you sign in. Autoplay, the speed you chose in the player, and the "plays on YouTube only" marks stay on each device.

### The video path: `data/watch.js`

The path was made once, by a script, from the module notes and the YouTube playlists, and is kept in `data/watch.js`. For each module, a curator read every recommended video and playlist and the module's note about it, and picked exactly the videos the note recommends, in the module's order, leaving out trailers and announcements; a second reviewer then checked every pick against the note. A playlist that several modules use is split by topic, so each module plays its own part. Each video plays once in the whole path: a video that an earlier module already plays is listed as "also" (it still counts for its course in the later module). Courses on other sites come at the end of their module. The path made on 17 November 2026 has 1,345 videos (about 738 hours) in 27 modules, plus 45 stops at courses on other sites (36 different courses: a few come back in later modules). The video lengths come from the playlists' public pages on YouTube and from YouTube's own player.

```js
window.AIM.watch = { v: 1, built: '2026-11-17', mods: [            // modules in course order, Level 1 to 4
  { id: 'l1-ai-basics', steps: [                                    // a module id from data/course.js
    { r: '<resource id>', k: 'v', vids: [['<video id>', 'Title', 600]] },            // one video
    { r: '<resource id>', k: 'l', list: '<playlist id>', vids: [[...], [...]], also: ['<video id>'] }, // part of a playlist
    { r: '<resource id>', k: 'p', vids: [[...], [...]] },           // one lesson in a few parts
    { r: '<resource id>', k: 'x' }                                  // a course on another site (no videos)
  ] }
] };
```

`r` is a resource in `data/resources.js`; each video is `[YouTube video id, title, length in seconds]` (0 when the length is not known: the page then guesses it from the average, and says "about"). `node scripts/check-data.js` checks the file: modules in course order, resources that exist, the step kinds (a video step must be a YouTube video or playlist, and a course on another site must not be one), 11-character video ids, no video twice, every "also" video played earlier, something of its own to play in every module, and no Devanagari in titles. A small fix by hand (for example, taking out a video that was removed from YouTube) is fine if it keeps this format; run the check after it.

## Reminders

Get a gentle reminder to study, at a time you choose. Missing a day costs nothing. Open **My progress** and press **Set up reminders** (the Study room has a Reminders card too).

- **Phone notifications.** Choose a time and days, which page opens when you tap the reminder (Daily review or Play mode), and whether to skip days when you have already studied. Press **Turn on reminders** and choose **Allow**. **Send a test** checks that they arrive.
  - **Android:** works in Chrome, even when Chrome is closed.
  - **iPhone and iPad:** needs iOS 16.4 or newer. ## Put the site online with GitHub Pages (free, about 5 minutes)

GitHub Pages hosts this folder as a website at no cost.

1. Open the repository on GitHub: `https://github.com/digitalmarket-arch/AI-ML`.
2. Make sure the site files are on the branch you want to publish. They were pushed to the branch `ccr-d2e52eb0-624rlk`. To publish from `main`, merge that branch into `main` first, using a pull request (a request to combine one branch into another).
3. Go to **Settings → Pages**.
4. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**.
5. Choose the branch (`main`, or `ccr-d2e52eb0-624rlk`) and the folder **/ (root)**. Press **Save**.
6. Wait 1–2 minutes and refresh the page. GitHub shows the address, usually `https://digitalmarket-arch.github.io/AI-ML/`.

> **Why not just double-click `index.html`?** You can, and most of the site works. But YouTube often refuses to play embedded videos on pages opened as files (it needs a proper web address), and some browsers block the in-browser Python there. On GitHub Pages everything works.

### Turn on sign-in (optional, free, about 10 minutes)

**This copy is already connected** to the Firebase project `ai-marg`, so sign-in is live on the GitHub Pages site. The steps below are for anyone who copies the site and wants sign-in on their own project.

Without this, the site works fully and each learner's progress stays in their own browser. With it, learners can press **Sign in** (top right), sign in with Google, and their progress is backed up and synced between their phone and laptop. It uses Firebase from Google on the free Spark plan (₹0; no card needed).

1. Go to [console.firebase.google.com](https://console.firebase.google.com), sign in with your Google account and press **Create a project**. Name it, for example `ai-marg`. You can turn Google Analytics off.
2. In the project, press the **web** icon (`</>`) to **add a web app**. Give it a nickname, leave "Firebase Hosting" unticked, and press **Register app**. Firebase shows a `firebaseConfig` with `apiKey`, `authDomain`, `projectId` and `appId`. Keep this page open.
3. Open **Build → Authentication → Get started → Sign-in method**, choose **Google**, switch it on, pick a support email and **Save**.
4. Still in Authentication, open **Settings → Authorized domains → Add domain** and add `digitalmarket-arch.github.io` (your GitHub Pages address without `https://`).
5. Open **Build → Firestore Database → Create database**. If asked, pick the **Standard** edition and keep the database ID as `(default)` (the site looks for that name). Choose a location in India (for example **asia-south1, Mumbai**) and start in **production mode**.
6. In Firestore, open the **Rules** tab, delete what is there, paste the whole of [`firestore.rules`](firestore.rules) from this repository, and press **Publish**. These rules let each learner read and write only their own progress.
7. Open `assets/js/firebase-config.js` in this repository (on GitHub you can press the pencil icon to edit it) and replace `window.AIM_FIREBASE = null;` with your values, for example:

   ```js
   window.AIM_FIREBASE = { apiKey: "AIza…", authDomain: "ai-marg.firebaseapp.com", projectId: "ai-marg", appId: "1:…:web:…" };
   ```

   Commit the change. A minute later the **Sign in** button appears on the site.
8. Optional but recommended: open **Project settings → General → Public settings** and set the **public-facing name** to your site's name (for example `AI Marg`). Otherwise Google may show a project number such as `project-519581742822` when learners sign in.

These config values are not secret (every Firebase web app shows them to the browser); the rules from step 6 are what keep each learner's data private. The free plan allows about 50,000 reads and 20,000 writes a day. That covers a few hundred daily learners because the site syncs a few seconds after a change (at most twice a minute), sends a playing video's position only every few minutes or when the learner switches away from the tab, and skips the write when nothing has changed.

**Privacy:** the cloud copy holds each learner's course progress and Google account ID; their name and email are visible to you in the Firebase console. Learners can delete their cloud copy themselves from the Account page; that removes their progress but not their sign-in record, so if someone asks you to delete everything, also delete them under **Authentication → Users**. If you run this for real users in India, keep a short privacy notice in line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (what you collect, why, and how to ask for deletion).

### Turn on AI help: the "Ask Gemini" buttons (optional, free tier, about 15 minutes)

Every learner already gets **Copy prompt for your AI app** next to the study tools: it copies a ready-made prompt for ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or any AI app (₹0, nothing to set up). This step adds an **Ask Gemini** button inside the site for signed-in learners, using Google's Gemini through your Firebase project (Firebase AI Logic). Sign-in (above) must be on first.

1. In the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com), open your project, then **Build → AI Logic → Get started**, and choose the **Gemini Developer API** (it works on the free Spark plan, with no billing). Follow the guided steps. If they offer to set up App Check, accept.
2. Create a reCAPTCHA key (it proves requests really come from your site): open the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com) with the same project selected, search for **reCAPTCHA** in the top bar, press **Create key**, choose **Website**, add the domain `digitalmarket-arch.github.io`, and create it. Copy the key ID. The free tier covers 10,000 checks a month without billing.
3. Back in Firebase, open **Build → App Check → Apps**, pick your web app, choose **reCAPTCHA Enterprise**, paste the key and save. Under **App Check → APIs**, check that **Firebase AI Logic** is **Enforced** (Firebase now turns this on for AI Logic).
4. Edit `assets/js/firebase-config.js` and change the `window.AIM_AI` line to:

   ```js
   window.AIM_AI = { enabled: true, model: "gemini-2.5-flash-lite", dailyPerPerson: 10, appCheckSiteKey: "PASTE-YOUR-KEY-ID", appCheckProvider: "enterprise" };
   ```

   Commit the change. A minute later, signed-in learners see **Ask Gemini** buttons (for example on a module's **Reflect** tab).

**Limits and cost:** the free tier gives the whole site a limited number of Gemini replies a day (the limit is per project, not per learner; check the current figure for your project in [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com)). `dailyPerPerson` stops one learner from using it all; it is counted in the learner's browser, so App Check is what really protects the quota. When the quota runs out, learners see a plain message and can still use Copy prompt. On the Spark plan there is no bill. If Google retires the model, change `model` to a current one.

**Privacy:** whatever a learner sends with Ask Gemini goes to Google, and on the free tier Google may use it to improve its products. The site says this next to the buttons; mention it in your privacy notice too.

The site works as plain files (GitHub Pages): read-aloud uses the voices on the learner's own device, and the Hinglish switch uses the built-in converter. `server.js` adds two extras when the site runs on a server with a Gemini API key (for example in Google AI Studio, or `npm install` then `GEMINI_API_KEY=... npm start` on your own computer):

### Reminder service (for the site owner)

Phone reminders need a small sender, because a website cannot wake a phone by itself. It runs on Cloudflare's free plan and needs no card.

**Put it online** (about 5 minutes, no coding):

[![Deploy to Cloudflare](https://deploy.workers.cloudflare.com/button)](https://deploy.workers.cloudflare.com/?url=https://github.com/digitalmarket-arch/AI-ML/tree/ccr-d2e52eb0-624rlk/reminders)

1. Create a free Cloudflare account at [dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up](https://dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up). It needs no card.
2. **Check your workers.dev name.** In the Cloudflare dashboard, open **Workers & Pages** in the menu. Find your *subdomain*: a name that ends in `.workers.dev` (it is shown next to **Your subdomain**, or under **Settings**). Cloudflare picks it for you, often from your email address, and it becomes part of a public address. If it shows your name or any part of your email, press **Change**, type a neutral name such as `ai-marg` (if that is taken, try `ai-marg-learn`), and save.
3. Press the **Deploy to Cloudflare** button above.
4. Sign in, and allow Cloudflare's GitHub app on your account when it asks. (Cloudflare copies the `reminders/` folder into a new repository in your GitHub account and builds it from there.)
5. Keep the suggested names, then press **Create and deploy**.
6. Wait for it to finish. Copy the address that ends in `.workers.dev` and send it to whoever maintains the site, so it can be put in `assets/js/remind.js`. Send the name of the new GitHub repository Cloudflare made too. Neither of them is a secret. If someone else maintains the site, also give them access to that repository (on GitHub: the repository's **Settings**, **Collaborators**, **Add people**), so they can send it fixes.

There are no keys or passwords to copy: the deploy makes the database, and the Worker makes its own tables and its own signing keys (VAPID keys) and keeps them in that database. The private key never leaves Cloudflare and is never in this repository. Opening the address by itself shows `{"ok":false,"error":"origin"}`. That is normal: the service only answers the AI Marg site. To check that it runs, open the address with `/v1/health` at the end (no slash after it): it shows `{"ok":true,"version":"1"}`. A brand-new address can take a few minutes to start working. Developers can deploy from a terminal instead: `cd reminders && npm install && npx wrangler deploy`. More in [reminders/README.md](reminders/README.md).

- **Switch it on in the site.** First check that the address holds no personal name or email; if it does, ask the owner to change the workers.dev name (step 2) and send the new address. Then put the Worker's address at the top of `assets/js/remind.js`, in place of `REMINDERS_URL` (`window.AIM_REMIND = window.AIM_REMIND || { url: 'https://....workers.dev' };`), and publish. The page asks the Worker for its public key by itself. Until then, the Reminders page says phone notifications are not switched on yet and offers the calendar reminder.
- **What runs where.** The Reminders page (`assets/js/remind.js`) and the service worker that shows the notifications (`sw.js`) are part of this site on GitHub Pages. The sender is the Cloudflare Worker in `reminders/`, with a small Cloudflare D1 database of the devices that turned reminders on. Every minute it sends the reminders that are due, through the push services of Google, Apple, Mozilla and Microsoft.
- **Free limits.** Cloudflare's free plan gives a Worker 100,000 requests a day and 10 ms of CPU time per run, and D1 5 GB with 100,000 row writes a day. The Worker sends at most 10 reminders a minute (14,400 a day) and keeps at most 20,000 devices (`BATCH` and `MAX_SUBS` in `reminders/wrangler.toml`), so it stays inside them. If many learners pick the same minute, some reminders arrive a few minutes late; one that is still waiting two hours after its time is skipped rather than sent at the wrong time of day (the same after an outage). If more than about 1,200 reminders are due at the same minute (most likely at the default 7:00 pm), some are more than two hours late and are skipped. Learners at that minute take turns, so the misses are shared out rather than falling on the same learners every day. Reminders due in the two hours after that minute wait behind it, so they can come up to two hours late, and the latest of them can be skipped too. Then raise `BATCH` (at most 18): see [reminders/README.md](reminders/README.md).
- **Deploy again.** The service runs from the new repository the button made in your GitHub account, not from `reminders/` in this repository. A change pushed to that repository deploys by itself and keeps the same database and keys. A fix made in `reminders/` here takes effect only once it is copied into that repository, which needs access to it (step 6). If that database is ever deleted, the Worker makes new keys, and learners must turn reminders off and on again.
- **Keep `sw.js` where it is.** Never rename or delete it: Safari drops the reminders of a site whose service worker file has gone.
- **App icons** (`assets/icons/`, used for the Home Screen and the notifications) are made by `python3 scripts/make-icons.py`.

## Editing the course

All content lives in the `data/` folder as JavaScript files that hold JSON (a simple text format for data). You can edit them in any text editor or directly on GitHub (open the file, then click the pencil icon).

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `data/course.js` | Levels and modules: titles, lesson-card text (overviews, study plans, key terms…), which lab belongs where |
| `data/examples/` | The examples on each module's lesson cards (one file per module) |
| `data/resources.js` | Every video, course and book, with language, level and the modules it serves |
| `data/quizzes.js` | Module quizzes (keyed by module id) and level tests (`test-l1` … `test-l4`) |
| `data/exercises.js` | In-browser Python exercises |
| `data/projects.js` | Projects and capstones |
| `data/glossary.js` | Jargon buster |
| `data/pages.js` | "Start here", career guide, FAQ and sources |
| `data/watch.js` | Watch mode's video path: every recommended video, module by module, in the order it plays. It was made once from the module notes and the playlists; edit it by hand (format under [Watch mode](#the-video-path-datawatchjs)) |

### Add a video or course

Copy an existing entry in `data/resources.js` and change the values:

```js
{
 "id": "campusx-100-days-of-machine-learning",   // unique, lowercase, dashes
 "title": "100 Days of Machine Learning",
 "creator": "CampusX",
 "platform": "YouTube",
 "type": "playlist",                  // playlist, video, course, channel, book, practice, dataset, article, tool
 "url": "https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=...",
 "firstVideoUrl": "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=...",   // optional: the first video (a watch?v= link, not the playlist); lets the site show a thumbnail
 "language": "hinglish",              // hindi, hinglish, indian-english, english
 "level": "beginner",                 // beginner, intermediate, advanced, expert
 "modules": ["l2-ml-foundations"],    // module ids from data/course.js
 "why": "One or two sentences on why it is good.",
 "status": "verified",                // "verified" only after you open the link yourself; "partial" or "unverified" shows a warning chip
 "listId": "", "videoId": ""          // optional; filled from the URLs automatically
 // "parts": ["videoId1", "videoId2"]  // optional: one resource in a few separate videos; plays them in order and ticks after all
}
```

Watch mode does not pick up a new video by itself. To play it there too, add a step for it to its module in `data/watch.js` (the format is under [The video path](#the-video-path-datawatchjs)), then run `node scripts/check-data.js`.

### Edit a lesson card

Each module in `data/course.js` has seven lesson cards: `overview` (The big idea), `outcomes`, `studySteps`, `keyTerms`, `indiaExample` (In India), `commonMistakes` and `selfCheck`. Write them in simple English: short sentences, and explain every technical word the first time a card uses it.

- **Hindi words in brackets** help Indian learners. Write them in both scripts, exactly like this: `average (औसत, ausat)`. Use them only for harder words, the first time they appear on a card. Hindi is not allowed anywhere else in the course text, and `node scripts/check-data.js` reports any that is.
- **Key terms** matter most: explain each one so a beginner gets it. Keep them in the same order (the Study room's review cards point to a key term by its position). Give a plain `meaning` (one or two short sentences), `like` (an everyday comparison; the site shows "Think of it like:" in front of it) and `example` (a tiny concrete example; for a coding term, one line of code and what it does). `"hi"` shows the Hindi word next to the term:

```js
{"term": "Variable", "hi": "चर, char", "meaning": "A name that holds a value, so you can use it later.",
 "like": "A steel dabba with a label: the label is the name, what you put inside is the value.",
 "example": "`price = 250` puts 250 in a box called price; `price * 2` then gives 500."}
```
- **Examples:** every card has at least 5 short examples, each with a title and one to three sentences. They live in `data/examples/<module id>.js` (loaded only when that module's lesson cards open, so every other page stays light), and the module in `data/course.js` has `"examples": true`:

```js
window.AIM.examples["l1-ai-basics"] = {
 "overview": [{"title": "Kirana shop", "text": "Riya's shop in Pune uses last year's bills to guess (अनुमान, anumaan) how much atta to order before Diwali."}]
};
```

- **Diagrams** for a module live in `assets/figures/<module id>.js`. `after` names the card a diagram belongs to. A card shows its text, then its diagrams, then its examples.

### Add a quiz question

In `data/quizzes.js`, add to the module's list. `answer` counts from 0, so 0 means the first option.

```js
{"id": "l2-regression-q11", "q": "What does a learning rate control?", "code": "",
 "options": ["Step size", "Number of features", "Test size", "Random seed"],
 "answer": 0, "explain": "It is the size of each step downhill...", "difficulty": "easy"}
```

### Add a coding exercise

Exercises in `data/exercises.js` have `starter` code, a reference `solution`, and `tests` written as `check(...)` lines:

```python
check("with_gst(1000, 18) returns 1180", lambda: close(with_gst(1000, 18), 1180), "Multiply by (1 + rate/100).")
```

`check(name, test, hint)` shows a green tick or a red cross with the hint. `close(a, b)` compares numbers or arrays with a small tolerance.

### Check your edits

If you have [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) installed, run this before you publish:

```bash
node scripts/check-data.js
```

It catches broken ids, missing fields, wrong answer numbers and missing lab files. A missing comma or quote shows up as "does not load". It also checks that Play mode, Watch mode and the reminders (`assets/js/remind.js`, `sw.js`) stay plain English and plain ES5, checks Watch mode's video path (`data/watch.js`), and checks that every icon in `manifest.webmanifest` is there.

## How the links were chosen and checked

- Research agents searched for the best free resources per module, Hindi/Hinglish first. A separate checker then re-found every link independently and confirmed the creator, language and cost.
- A link marked **verified** was seen exactly in a search result or a creator's own GitHub page in **October 2026**. Links that could not be fully confirmed carry a chip: "link unconfirmed" when the link itself could not be confirmed, and "partly confirmed" when the link was found but a detail (such as the uploader, or whether free audit is still offered) could not be checked. On YouTube, those cards also get a "Search YouTube" button.
- YouTube playlists change over time. If something breaks, use the "Report it on GitHub" link in the footer, or fix it in `data/resources.js`.

## How it is built

- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. There is no build step and no framework, so it runs on any static host.
- Python runs in the browser through Pyodide 0.29.5, loaded from `cdn.jsdelivr.net` the first time a learner presses Run (about 10–30 MB, then cached).
- Fonts come from Google Fonts: Rozha One, Mukta and JetBrains Mono.
- Labs are self-contained scripts in `assets/js/labs/`. Each one registers itself on `window.AILabs`.
- Play mode is `assets/js/game.js` and `assets/css/game.css`. They load only when a Play page is opened, or a few seconds after the site opens for a learner who chose Play, so Learn pages are as fast as before. XP is worked out from the progress you already have, so Play adds no separate score that could drift from it.
- Watch mode is `assets/js/watch.js`, `assets/css/watch.css` and its video path `data/watch.js`. They load only when a Watch mode page is opened, so every other page is as fast as before. It plays the videos with YouTube's player API (the privacy-enhanced youtube-nocookie.com player that Learn already uses), and sends nothing anywhere else. The browser also keeps a second copy of Watch mode's progress (`aimarg.watch.part`), so a tab left open from before an update, which does not know Watch mode, does not erase newer Watch progress with its own saves. After Reset all progress, a sign-out that removes the progress from the device, or Restore from file (in any tab), that copy is never used again, so it never brings back progress that was removed. Doing one of those in such an old tab while signed in can lose Watch progress made in newer tabs since that tab was opened.
- Reminders are `assets/js/remind.js` (loaded only for the Reminders page, or a few seconds after the site opens in a browser where reminders are on), `sw.js` (it only shows notifications; it never caches the site), `manifest.webmanifest` (for the Home Screen) and the Worker in `reminders/`.
